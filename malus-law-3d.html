How it works

The law

Light from a lamp is unpolarized: its electric field points in every direction at random. A polarizer lets through only the part of the field along its transmission axis, so the light that comes out is polarized. When that light reaches a second polarizer, the analyzer, turned by an angle θ, only the field component along the analyzer’s axis gets through. That component is E₀ cos θ. Intensity goes as the square of the field, so:

I = I₀ cos²θ

Here I₀ is the intensity of the polarized light arriving at the analyzer, and θ is the angle between the two transmission axes.

The setup

  • Source (white sphere): emits unpolarized light. The white bars jump between random directions.
  • Polarizer (cerulean ring): passes one fixed direction only. The cerulean bar shows the polarized field.
  • Analyzer (rose ring): rotates by θ. The rose bar shrinks as cos θ, and fewer photons make it through.
  • Screen and meter: the screen brightness and the glowing bar show the transmitted intensity as a share of I₀.
